I'm Mary-Lynn Bragg, though my friends call me MLB. I'm a strategic growth and partnerships leader. I help organizations evaluate and develop the relationships and opportunities that open new and larger paths to revenue.

## Who I help

Early-stage, founder-led organizations, usually at the point where the founder is still the partnership function. Founder-led sales got you here, and the first partnerships came from the founder's own network and instincts. That works until it doesn't scale, and the founder needs to step out of the day-to-day without losing what those relationships are worth.

My experience is in EdTech. The work may apply more broadly.

## What I'm known for

Looking at a partnership roster and working out which relationships have the strongest profit and growth potential, which ones are quietly costing more than they return, and which ones nobody has thought to build yet. Then building the plan and the systems to grow revenue through stronger relationships, so it doesn't all live in one person's head.

## How I got here

I started in library and information science, which turned out to be good training for finding signal in messy systems. I moved into education technology at ProQuest SIPX, where I built the implementation and support function for 26 institutions and 340,000 student users, and every one of them renewed. At Ex Libris I ran campus engagement and built a customer health score that surfaced at-risk accounts before they left.

In 2019 I joined Chegg and stayed nearly six years. I built the first B2B partner success function for Chegg Skills, and the first partnership grew from zero to $18.5M in its first year, 390% over goal. Across four companies I've built customer-facing success and growth organizations from the ground up, including a full build at a VC-backed EdTech startup.

The titles changed a lot. The job underneath didn't: working out which relationships matter, and building what it takes to grow them.

## How I work

- **Look at the whole roster before adding to it.** Most partnerships get built one handshake at a time. I start by finding out which ones are earning their place.
- **Find where the potential concentrates.** It is usually in a few relationships, and often not the loudest ones.
- **Turn instinct into a system.** What a founder does by feel becomes a plan someone else can run.
- **Test before committing.** I design the cheapest way to learn whether a partnership will pay off before anyone bets heavily on it.
- **Stay accountable to revenue.** A strong relationship that doesn't grow the business is a pleasant lunch.
- **Use AI to move faster, never to hand off a decision.** I've written down [how I use it](/ai-use-disclosure/).

> Most partnership rosters are built one handshake at a time. Someone should eventually check which handshakes are paying off.
