I'm Mary-Lynn Bragg, though my friends call me MLB. I help organizations work out where to grow next, and then I stay to make it real.

I'm drawn to the stage where an opportunity looks promising and nobody can yet say whether it's real. A new market, a different business model, a partnership that might become a channel or might become a distraction. Most growth plans treat that uncertainty as a formality. I treat it as the work: find the pattern, name the bets hiding inside it, and design the cheapest way to learn which ones are wrong.

## How I got here

I started in library and information science, which turned out to be good training for finding signal in messy systems. I moved into education technology at ProQuest SIPX, where I built the implementation and support function for 26 institutions and 340,000 student users, and every one of them renewed. At Ex Libris I ran campus engagement and built a customer health score that surfaced at-risk accounts before they left.

In 2019 I joined Chegg and stayed nearly six years. I built the first B2B partner success function for Chegg Skills, and the first partnership grew from zero to $18.5M in its first year, 390% over goal. I helped move the business from direct-to-consumer to enterprise, and I worked across market strategy, partnerships, customer success, and sales leadership along the way.

The titles changed a lot. The job underneath didn't. I've mostly played the role that sits between departments: connecting ideas across teams, seeing patterns others miss, and turning a tangle into a clear next step.

## What I care about most

Strategy that gets tested. A plan nobody checks against reality is a guess with a good deck. I would rather be proven wrong early, by something small, than be right on paper.

## How I work

- **Start with what would have to be true.** Every bet rests on assumptions. I write them down first.
- **Run the cheapest test that could prove me wrong.** A small, fast experiment beats a long debate.
- **Get close to customers and the market before forming opinions.** Signal first, then a point of view.
- **Stay for the execution.** Judgment only matters if someone owns what happens next.
- **Use AI to move faster, never to hand off a decision.** I've written down [how I use it](/ai-use-disclosure/).

> A growth plan is a set of bets. The skill is knowing which ones to double down on, and finding out early which ones to drop.

## Where I think this is going

AI is changing what customer success and partnership teams are for. As more of the routine work gets automated, the human part of a relationship matters more, not less: who to trust, what to promise, when to walk away. The organizations that work this out early will build something their competitors can't copy by buying the same software.
